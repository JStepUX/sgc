# Changelog — 2026-09

## 2026-09-14 — Retrieval cues from the state turn, and `persistent` means something again (spec 09)

- **Why**: the first live session on spec 08 S3 showed the summary corpus
  quiet for a structural reason. The state turn writes summaries in an
  abstract register ("shift from visceral tension to philosophical
  affinity"), so a later question in the person's own words ("who do I
  admire?") shares no token with them, and "I'm a huge fan of Alan Watts"
  had landed nowhere durable: spec 07 had retired `persistent` to the
  continuity sheet, whose character slots are scene-shaped.
- **Cues** (C1): the state turn may emit up to six short strings per turn —
  OTHER words a person might later use for the exchange, never words already
  in it — which the summary corpus indexes and no prompt renders (the one
  visible trace is `via summary "…"` provenance, and only as a stem the
  person typed). Mirrors what brains already do with aliases. Bounded by a
  novel-stem budget (12) rather than a character count, because one wrong
  stem in a 40-token summary clears the gate on its own and twenty stems
  dilute a real match by a quarter (`lib/turn-parser.ts` coerceCues,
  `lib/constants.ts`). Shown in the inspector's summary card.
- **Persistent** (C2, reverses spec 07's retirement deliberately): attributed,
  dated statements the person made about themselves or their own world
  ("said they're a huge fan of Alan Watts") — rendered in the distilled
  buffer for two turns, then retrievable through the summary corpus. Not
  current truth: a retraction is a separate statement and nothing reconciles
  them (documented limit). Scene conditions still belong on the sheet;
  demonstrated behaviour still belongs in established patterns.
- **Hardening that fell out of the review**: hydrated summaries are now
  normalised through the same coercer as fresh ones (a hand-edited or
  oversized blob can't reach the index unbounded); a summary with cues but
  nothing to render is not indexed; a JSON repair that had to close a
  truncated string now marks it, and the half-finished list item is dropped
  instead of persisted as a fact; a `both` hit carries the summary side's
  own matched terms so a cue that caused the appendage is visible in the
  detail modal.
- **Measured, for the record** (real chat as corpus, a made-up name injected
  once): a novel name pings hard in short and median turns; in a 200+-token
  turn a one- or two-word name mentioned once sits under the gate for both
  engines, and the same name in a 28-token summary line clears it. Hence
  names belong in summary lines, and cues are for other words only.
- Eval: cues on the summaries fixture, two probes (a label only in cues
  retrieves; a wrong cue never outranks the real hit). Ratchet unchanged.

## 2026-09-13 — Summary corpus: a second retrieval index over the state turn's summaries (spec 08 S3)

- **The memory grep now searches two corpora**: the raw turn text (as
  before) and the per-turn summaries the state turn already writes
  (`buildSummaryDocs`, `lib/tfidf.ts`), fused raw-first in `searchScored`.
  Why: the lab's ceiling case — ordinary-noun callbacks inside one long
  scene ("the broken ribs", "our trip") — is unreachable for any lexical
  engine when the raw prose never restates the label, and the summary
  states it in plain words. Ranking stays pure math over both corpora;
  no new call — the summaries already exist for the distilled buffer.
- **Fusion rule (James's ruling)**: raw hits keep their rank and body; a
  summary hit only fills a slot raw left empty and never outranks a raw
  hit — the two corpora keep separate statistics (summary docs ~40 tokens,
  raw ~300) and their scores are not comparable. A turn matched in both is
  one result, the raw pair with the summary lines beneath it: the meat is
  in the turn, the summary is the pointer. Summary-only hits render as
  `[Turn N · when · via summary "…"] Summary:` plus bulleted lines — never
  the raw text, which Sal hasn't read.
- **This is a raise, stated as one.** Summaries are model-distilled text,
  so the model's choice of what to compress now decides which older turns
  are discoverable by label. Summaries are not user-editable; the control
  surface is per-message gating (a pair whose *user* half is switched off
  is excluded from the summary corpus too — a switched-off fact must not
  resurface through Sal's summary of it), plus two knobs in
  `lib/constants.ts`: `SUMMARY_CONCEPT_THRESHOLD` (0.08) and
  `SUMMARY_CORPUS_MIN_DOCS` (5 — below that every term is rare and IDF says
  nothing).
- **Recall tool**: a turn Sal has seen only as a summary pointer stays
  expandable — a raw match on it is new information, not a duplicate, and
  `around_turn` on it fetches the turn itself as well as its neighbours.
  The recall loop tracks that set alongside its dedup set.
- **Inspector**: citation cards preview the pointer for summary-only hits;
  the detail modal badges `via summary` / `turn + summary` and shows the
  served summary lines. Persisted on `GrepDetail` (optional; older rows
  read as raw turns).
- **Eval**: a `summaries` fixture and two probes — a label present only in
  a summary retrieves; the same turn by its raw words is still won by the
  raw corpus. Ratchet unchanged.
- Local replay lab: dev-DB chats now load their summaries, `--no-summaries`
  gives the raw-only baseline (lab is gitignored; never lands).

## 2026-09-13 — Time intent: stop chrono firing on narrative text (spec 08 S2)

- **The time-intent parser now filters chrono's matches before anchoring**
  (`parseTimeIntent`, `lib/time-score.ts`). Pre-existing bug, surfaced by the
  replay lab: chrono is eager on narrative text, and in a ~65-turn roleplay
  38 of 65 user turns carried an "intent" — a character named April (anchor
  → the 1st of April), "4 years ago" (−1461 d), scene words like "night" and
  "evening". Each anchored the time score months or years from every
  candidate, so combined ≈ 0 for all of them and results survived only via
  the concept rescue, ranked by pool order. Three rules, on chrono's own
  certainty flags: **R1** keep a match only if its day or weekday is certain,
  or its year and month both are (drops bare months, bare years, and
  nothing-certain forms like "this morning"); **R3** drop the four
  day-certain present-tense phrases (now, right now, today, tonight — costs
  nothing in ranking order); **R2** roll a year-uncertain anchor that lands
  beyond next week back a year (`forwardDate: false` is "not forced forward",
  not "past" — "May 1" asked in January meant last May), then reject an
  anchor outside the span of turns that exist. Keeps yesterday, last night,
  last Monday, 3 days ago, last week, a month ago, May 1.
- **Accepted limits, written down at the parser**: "in April" no longer
  anchors (a month-wide intent under a 7-day decay from the 1st was a poor
  anchor anyway); narrative "yesterday" / "we leave tomorrow" still pass —
  no lexical rule can tell them from a recall cue, and they mis-steer by
  days, not years. The sanctioned next step if that bites is a
  recall-context gate (spec 08 `decisions`).
- `TimeIntent` gains `rejected` — the first phrase the filters dropped — for
  tests and the replay lab; nothing renders it yet.
- Tests pin the **real chrono output** for every rule (`time-score.test.ts`),
  including two inputs the draft spec assumed would parse and don't ("we
  have at least 4 years", "on the 9th").

## 2026-09-13 — Grep engine: cosine × BM25 product (spec 08 S1)

- **The memory grep now ranks turns by cosine × squashed BM25** instead of
  cosine alone (`CONCEPT_ENGINE` in `lib/constants.ts`, the one site that
  chooses; `lib/bm25.ts` is the new engine + dispatcher, scoring the same
  turn corpus `lib/tfidf.ts` builds). Still pure math, no model. Why: cosine
  measures resemblance, so two turns set in the same scene ("bed", "sheet",
  "eyes") looked alike whatever happened in them and scene furniture could
  carry a pull on its own. BM25's IDF goes to ~0 for a word in every turn,
  so multiplying the two lets each veto the other's failure mode. Evidence
  from a local replay lab over four labelled roleplay corpora (21–65 turns):
  anchor recall equal to cosine on all four, junk-pull rate 0.62 → 0.24 on
  the long-paragraph corpus, never worse. BM25 *ordering* was tried and
  withdrawn — only its veto is robust. The lab (`scripts/eval/`) is
  gitignored: its gold files quote people's chats.
- **Eval ratchet** (`retrieval-eval.test.ts`, 16 pass-probes): recall@3
  1.000 / MRR 1.000 on the product engine (was 1.000 / 0.969 on cosine —
  the one rank slip from the stemming landing is gone). Ratchet unchanged
  at 0.90.
- **Documented trades, pinned by tests** (`bm25.test.ts`, `time-score.test.ts`):
  below ~10 retrievable turns the furniture veto is weak (the gap holds, the
  absolute doesn't); a word present in *every* turn retrieves nothing (cosine
  returned an arbitrary tie); one rare word mentioned once inside a long turn
  falls under the gate (cosine had it at 0.10, itself marginal).
- **Inspector**: the retrieval detail modal's meta line now reads
  `concept 0.xxx (cosine 0.xx × bm25 0.xx) × time 0.xx` — both components
  persist on `GrepDetail` (optional; older rows show the bare concept score).
- **Honest comment fix, no behaviour change**: `searchScored`'s header claimed
  two independent gates. Under production defaults (time weight 1.0, both
  thresholds 0.08) the filter reduces to concept ≥ 0.08 and time re-ranks
  within the survivors — pre-existing since the time scorer landed, now
  written down instead of implied.

## 2026-09-07 — Continuity sheet: durable scene facts the buffers drop (spec 07)

- **Why.** `TurnSummary.persistent` ("true until explicitly changed") reached
  the prompt for four entries — two verbatim, two distilled — then never
  again, and the grep couldn't bring a shirt back because a shirt is never the
  topic. Salience gating's structural blind spot: durable, low-salience facts.
  Design agreed in session with a selection criterion (holds until an event
  changes it, later replies must stay consistent with it, neither buffer nor a
  topical search could reconstruct it) and a fixed schema so the model never
  judges what's worth tracking — the schema asks, empty slots stay empty.
- **Storage is deltas, the sheet is a fold (post-review).** Codex's
  adversarial review found the first cut's storage wrong: each turn stored its
  whole sheet, and with no composer gate two overlapping state calls could
  land in either order, so whichever landed later silently dropped the
  other's delta — fatal for an accreting record (fine for regenerated inner
  state). Now each assistant entry stores only its own delta
  (`ChatEntry.sheetDelta`), and every reader — the assembler, the rail, the
  state prompt's previous sheet — calls `foldSheet(log)`, which applies the
  deltas in log order. Landing order is irrelevant by construction, an edited
  turn's contribution vanishes the moment its delta is cleared (the edit path
  now clears it, blob and memory — also a review finding), and a corrupted
  persisted delta applies nothing instead of crashing a render. Also from the
  review: template placeholders like `<only if it changed>` echoed back by a
  small model are no-ops (a value, a name, a location name); the "use null"
  instruction is scoped to the internal state so a null continuity slot is
  unambiguously a deletion; the raw-text extractor only accepts a key in key
  position; a cleanly-parsing response supplies the delta from the parsed
  object (escaped keys included). Two findings were dissents and stayed as
  documented exceptions: returning a character clears a stale
  `absence_reason`, and naming a previously unnamed place merges rather than
  replaces. Detail in spec 07 `review_2026-09-07`.
- **The sheet (`src/client/lib/continuity.ts`).** story {genre, time},
  location {name, type, environment}, characters keyed by normalised name
  {present, absence_reason, immediate_location, stance, apparel, items,
  physical_state, disposition_to_user, unaware_of}. Caps: 160 chars/slot, 16
  records. `applyContinuityDelta` merges model deltas: omission never deletes;
  a character is never removed, only marked absent with a reason (returning
  clears the stale reason); a location whose name changes is replaced
  wholesale; null/blank clears a slot; non-strings, unknown fields and
  prototype-reaching keys are ignored; an unusable delta returns the previous
  sheet. Whitespace-collapsed so no slot can fabricate a prompt line.
- **Rides the state turn — no new call.** `parseStateResponse` lifts the
  `continuity` object from the response by string-aware brace matching of the
  RAW text, so a token cap that falls inside it yields no delta (a truncated
  apparel is a fact; `completeJson` may still salvage the summary and state).
  Continuity is the FIRST key of the schema so a cap lands after it.
  `callStateTurn` returns the delta as emitted; `commit` stamps `sheetDelta`
  on the entry and into `inspector_json` beside `dynamicState`. Both callers
  derive the prompt's `prevSheet` by folding the same log slice `prevState`
  comes from, so a re-spin's sheet is the one that turn saw. The state prompt renders the previous sheet as JSON
  in the system half (data, not instructions) so the model reuses record keys;
  the copy is written against the criterion — genre-neutral, no scenario
  nouns (pinned by test). The summary prompt no longer asks for `persistent`;
  the key stays in the shape so the type and old chats are untouched.
- **Read path.** `ChatEntry.sheetDelta` / `TurnData.sheetDelta`,
  `sheetDeltaFromInspector` mirror in `turn-data.ts`, `foldSheet` in the
  assembler.
  `buildPrompt` renders it as CONTINUITY — labelled lines, never JSON —
  between RETRIEVED HISTORY and EARLIER CONTEXT; omitted when empty; never in
  the naive baseline. The rail gets a read-only Continuity card above Dynamic
  State showing the folded sheet, with a "no changes this turn" line when the
  latest delta is empty — `components/ContinuityCard.tsx`, its own file
  because the inline version pushed TurnInspector past the 500-line ratchet. No editor and no composer gate — ruled:
  the person is there to be in the story, correction is diegetic, and the
  next turn reads the newest completed sheet exactly as it does inner state.
- **S0 prerequisite (spec 06 S1).** `buildPrompt` now takes one
  `PromptInput` object; the 45 test call sites and both production callers
  converted; a 24-fixture snapshot of the rendered prompt was byte-identical
  before and after. Spec 06 S2–S5 remain unscheduled.
- **After a design chat with Astra (gpt-6-astra, read-only; transcript in
  `docs/ignored/`, untracked; digest in spec 07 `astra_chat_2026-09-07`).**
  Four small changes: the state call is retried once on a transport failure
  (never the write, never a malformed response — for an accreting record a
  lost reflection is a permanent omission, not one-turn staleness); the
  CONTINUITY block says scene facts are not character knowledge, and the spec
  now says an omitted `unaware_of` leaves knowledge *unspecified*; the rail
  distinguishes "no changes this turn" from "nothing recorded this turn"; and
  the re-spin comments no longer claim a sliced fold reproduces what the
  original turn saw (it is hindsight — late reflections included — unlike the
  pacing draw, which is replayed exactly). Deferred with names: add/remove
  semantics for `items`, present-newcomer eviction at the record cap, a
  catch-up rule, and — the recommended next piece — an evidence harness that
  traces one claim per scene through the whole loop on the local model.
- **Docs: why this exists.** CLAUDE.md's brief and a new README section
  record the project's origin question and its honest current scope (a
  narrative runtime built for fun), and stop calling the proxy "deliberately
  dumb" now that the paragraph cut is an editorial act on the server.
- **Not done, on purpose.** `established_patterns` still has the four-turn
  horizon; non-narrative chats get an empty sheet. Friction decides whether a
  general standing-facts section is warranted.

## 2026-09-03 — Reply pacing: a drawn paragraph ceiling, enforced at the break

- **Why.** 512-flat was wrong twice over: a small local model treats any cap
  as a target and fills it every turn (six paragraphs for a two-line beat),
  and a thinking model needs the cap roomy anyway because the `<think>` block
  comes out of the same budget. "How much progress to make this turn" is a
  judgement call the local models can't hold from a sentence of guidance,
  and sizing the reply off the person's input would read as mirroring
  (ruled out). So: random, bounded, enforced — and shown only after the fact.
- **The draw (`src/client/lib/pacing.ts`).** Each reply gets a paragraph
  ceiling from a weighted deck (1–5, weighted to 2–3, never the same twice
  running), blind to the input. Same shape as the spontaneity engine:
  injectable RNG, no module state — the no-repeat rule reads the previous
  ceiling off `latestTurn` (restored on load/undo/tangent like everything
  else there), so no new ref threads through the hooks. Snapshotted onto
  `TurnData` (`pacingCeiling` / `pacingOutcome` / `pacingTrimmed`, via a
  `PacingInspector` the type `extends`); a re-spin always REPLAYS the ceiling
  (it's the turn's bound, not a perturbation to undo); a hand edit keeps the
  ceiling and clears the outcome.
- **The prompt.** With a ceiling the tail's pacing line names the number
  ("within a ceiling of N paragraphs this turn: fewer is fine when the beat is
  done…"); without one, this morning's judgement-only wording. The shared
  opening stays a pinned tail marker.
- **The cut (`src/server/paragraph-cap.ts` + `turn-route.ts`).** Reply calls
  carry `maxParagraphs`; the route feeds every visible delta through a pure
  blank-line counter and, at the Nth break, aborts upstream and closes with
  stopReason `max_paragraphs`. No sentence detection anywhere — a break is
  `\n[ \t]*\n`, nothing else (ruled: sentence heuristics against RP prose are
  scar tissue). A trailing newline run is held back across fragments so a
  straddling break still counts; empty paragraphs never count; an
  under-ceiling reply is byte-identical to no cap. The state turn never sends
  the field, so its JSON is untouched. The regex is duplicated client/server
  (the server can't import client code) and both copies are pinned against
  the same fixtures.
- **The fallback (client, `api.ts`).** A paced call that hit `max_tokens`
  first is trimmed back to its last complete paragraph — client-side, since
  the half paragraph is already on the wire and deltas can't retract. No
  break at all → nothing trimmed, truncation left visible, inspector says so:
  one paragraph that outran the whole budget is a broken generation, not a
  pacing problem. Unpaced calls (the state turn) are never trimmed.
- **Inspector.** A `¶ ceiling N paragraphs · <outcome>` card on paced turns
  (after the fact only — the draw is meant to be unpredictable at the
  composer).
- **To verify on the machine, not in tests:** KoboldCPP runs one generation
  at a time. If it keeps generating after the route drops the connection at
  a cut, the state turn queues behind the phantom tail and summaries land
  late after every cut. Recent builds are believed to stop on disconnect; if
  they don't, the fix is one extra request to Kobold's abort endpoint on cut.
- Tests: 42 new — deck weights / no-repeat / dead-card sweep, trim cases
  (incl. dialogue + markdown punctuation left alone), the server counter
  (straddling breaks, held whitespace, empty paragraphs, char-at-a-time
  equivalence), the route end-to-end over real HTTP with a fake provider
  (cut + abort + done frame, under-ceiling passthrough, no-cap path, 400s),
  `runTurn` trim semantics, recall-loop forwarding, prompt line variants.
- **Codex review (Sol), three findings, all confirmed against the code and
  fixed:** (1) the hard-cap trim treated a break at offset 0 as a paragraph
  end and could persist an EMPTY reply for text opening with blank lines —
  now a break only counts with real text before it, same rule as the server
  counter; (2) a cut turn reported usage as a measured 0, which the
  Context-Savings tile read as 100% saved — the route now sends null, the
  client estimates over what actually went on the wire and flags it
  (`usageEstimated`; the tile labels Sent "(est.)"), and a local server that
  omits usage (the pre-existing silent-0 case) gets the same treatment; (3)
  the ceiling was per REQUEST, so a recall turn could spend it once per
  round — the recall loop now subtracts paragraphs already committed from
  earlier rounds (floor 1). Sol could not run Vitest in its read-only
  sandbox; typecheck passed there, the full suite passed here.
- **Re-spin can change the ceiling (dogfooding follow-up).** The edit-reply
  modal now carries a paragraph-ceiling picker (1–5, or none) beside the
  operator replay toggle, defaulting to the turn's own ceiling — a faithful
  replay unless changed. The saved turn records the ceiling that actually
  ran (`RespinResult.ceiling` → `pacingCeiling`), so the inspector card and
  the next draw's no-repeat rule see the truth. Motivation: the usual reason
  to re-spin a cut reply is that the draw was too small for the beat.
- **Seams logged, not fixed:** `docs/06_turn-pipeline-seams-spec.yaml` (status
  proposed) records the two seams this landing exposed — one per-turn field
  touches five shapes, and the two per-turn draws are hand-wired through
  runner, editor, assembler and prompt — with the trigger conditions (next
  per-turn field, third draw, Stop/Continue, a 15th buildPrompt param) and the
  ordered refactor. Nothing scheduled.

## 2026-09-03 — LOCAL path: thinking models (Qwen3 & co.) and the output cap

- **`<think>` blocks are separated at the provider boundary.** A reasoning
  model on the LOCAL provider used to stream its whole chain of thought into
  the thread: persisted as the reply, re-entering the local buffer and
  Grepory, and sitting in front of the state turn's JSON. `separateThinking`
  (`src/server/openai-provider.ts`) now splits raw content into visible reply
  + reasoning, once, for both producers (live turn and state turn). It is a
  pure, prefix-stable function of the content-so-far — a trailing partial tag
  is held back until the next fragment disambiguates it — so the append-only
  delta contract to the client holds. `delta.reasoning_content` /
  `delta.reasoning` (servers that split thinking for you) route to reasoning
  too. Reasoning is dropped, not surfaced (ruled: not interested in seeing it).
  One shape is deliberately unhandled: a template that pre-fills `<think>` so
  only a bare `</think>` arrives — undecidable mid-stream; the stray tag is
  dropped and the fix is the server-side split (llama.cpp `--reasoning-format`).
- **Thinking-only replies are an error, not a blank turn.** If the visible
  reply is empty but reasoning isn't, `streamTurn` throws an actionable
  message naming `LLM_MAX_TOKENS` (when `finish_reason` was `length`) instead
  of letting the buffers and state turn run on nothing. `finish_reason` is now
  mapped to the client vocabulary (`stop`→`end_turn`, `length`→`max_tokens`);
  it was hardcoded to `end_turn`, so a truncated reply looked finished.
- **`LLM_MAX_TOKENS` default 512 → 4096, and re-framed as a ceiling.** The
  thinking comes out of the same budget before the reply starts, so 512 never
  reached a reply on Qwen3. Copy in `.env.example`, `index.ts`, the desktop
  provider modal and README now says ceiling-not-target.
- **Reply length is steered by the prompt, not the cap.** Small local models
  treated 512 as a target and filled it every turn — six paragraphs for a
  two-line beat. A persona-neutral pacing line joined the architectural tail
  of `lib/prompt.ts` ("Size your reply to the moment…"), pinned as a tail
  marker in `prompt.test.ts`. This is every-turn prompt copy and therefore a
  behavioural change to BOTH providers (AGENTS.md rule) — it wants a LOCAL
  sanity pass on Qwen and an eye on Opus; gate it to LOCAL if Opus tightens.
- Tests: 20 new in `providers.test.ts` (separation contract incl. a
  character-by-character prefix-stability walk, split tags, held-back `<`,
  reasoning_content routing, finish_reason mapping, and the provider guard via
  a stubbed `fetch`).
